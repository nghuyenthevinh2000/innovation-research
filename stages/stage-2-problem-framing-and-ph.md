# Stage 2: Problem Representation & Cognitive Search

> **Phase Focus:** Applying Perspective-Heuristic (P-H) Pairs, Escaping Representational Inertia, and Harnessing Cognitive Diversity.

---

## 1. Stage Objective & Theoretical Foundation

Once a technology seed is isolated, founders must determine *how to represent the problem space*. The choice of coordinate system dictates whether commercialization will be computationally impossible, agonizingly slow, or effortlessly trivial.

> [!QUOTE] The Herbert Simon Principle
> *"Solving a problem simply means representing it so as to make the solution transparent."*
> — Herbert Simon (Nobel Laureate, 1978)

## 2. Key Concepts & Definitions

- **Perspective:** An internal representation or coordinate system of the problem at hand that dictates how the problem space is mapped, categorized, and visualized.
- **Heuristic:** A search algorithm, rule of thumb, or navigational strategy applied to a problem once it has been represented through a specific perspective to locate an optimal solution.
- **Perspective-Heuristic Pair (P-H Pair):** The coupled combination of a representation and a search rule.
  $$\text{Problem Difficulty} = f(\text{Perspective}, \text{Heuristic})$$
- **The Magic Square Analogy:** Herbert Simon proved that the high-cognitive-load arithmetic game "Number 15" is isomorphic to Tic-Tac-Toe when mapped into a 3×3 Magic Square. Re-mapping eliminates arithmetic computation entirely.
- **Cognitive Diversity:** Scott Page's *Diversity Trumps Ability Theorem* proves that a cognitively diverse group will consistently outperform a homogeneous group of high-ability domain experts because they bring disparate coordinate systems to the same landscape.

---

## 3. Core Transformation Activities

1. **Map Alternative Coordinate Systems:** For any technology seed, map at least 3–5 distinct perspectives before committing to a commercial path (e.g., framing 3D printing as desktop documentation vs. pharmaceutical pill fabrication vs. foundry die manufacturing).
2. **Import Cross-Domain Analogies:** Systematically borrow mature perspectives from distant scientific and industrial fields (e.g., Black-Scholes using heat transfer thermodynamics; DBS using electrical circuit oscillation).
3. **Establish Expert-Free Zones:** Create structured environments where non-experts can propose "unreasonable" cross-domain hypotheses without reputational risk.
4. **Deploy Crowdsourced Search:** Broadcast an abstracted functional problem formulation to an open, multidisciplinary network to break organizational boundary inertia.

---

## 4. Governing Mental Models & Failure Modes

- **Mental Model:** *The Magic Square Principle* — when stuck, never blame your intelligence or try to compute faster; step back and transform the coordinate system.
- **Cognitive Trap to Avoid:** *The Representational Trap (Functional Fixedness)* — staring at the technology exclusively through the lens of the academic department in which it was discovered.
- **Cognitive Trap to Avoid:** *The Late-Joiner Trap* — when a newcomer introduces an alternative perspective mid-stream, apply the *Structured Challenge Protocol* rather than engaging in defensive factional debates.

---

## 5. Stage Gate 2: Exit Deliverables

Before advancing to [**Stage 3: Needs-Seeds Synchronization**](./stage-3-needs-seeds-synchronization.md), the venture must possess:
- [ ] Documented **P-H Search Matrix** detailing at least 3 distinct market coordinate systems.
- [ ] Cross-domain analogy mapping identifying how analogous functional problems were solved in unrelated industries.
- [ ] Evaluation of cognitive diversity within the team (balancing domain specialists with cross-disciplinary outsiders).
- [ ] Explicit hypothesis selection justifying which P-H coordinate system will be taken into customer discovery.
