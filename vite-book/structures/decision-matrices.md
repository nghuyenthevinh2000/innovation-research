# Multi-Criteria Decision Matrices for Systematic Innovation

> **Decision Architectures for Commercialization Pathways, Capital Instruments, and Go-to-Market Strategies**

---

## 1. Commercialization Pathway Matrix

*Use in Stage 3 to decide whether to form a Product Firm, Service Provider, Platform, or Licensing entity.*

| Evaluation Criteria | Product Firm (Software / Device) | Service Firm (Bespoke Solutions) | Platform / Matchmaker (Multi-Sided) | Pure IP Licensing (Royalty Model) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Capital Intensity (CapEx)** | Medium to High (tooling / R&D) | Very Low (headcount-driven) | Medium (software build) | Minimal (lean patent filing lab) |
| **Gross Margin Potential** | 65% – 85% | 30% – 50% | 75% – 90% | 90% – 98% |
| **Scalability Velocity** | High (decoupled from headcount) | Constrained (tied to billable hours) | Exponential (network effects) | High (leveraging licensee factories) |
| **Defensibility & Moat** | Proprietary architecture & brand | Specialized team expertise | Data network effects & switching cost | Foundational patents & FTO barriers |
| **Time to First Revenue** | 12 – 24 months | 1 – 3 months | 18 – 36 months | 24 – 48 months (standards dependent) |
| **Execution Risk** | Manufacturing / distribution failure | Talent retention & utilization | Chicken-and-egg launch failure | Licensee apathy & non-enforcement |
| **Best Suited For:** | Packaged standardized solutions | Consultative exploratory deep tech | Ecosystems with disjoint buyers/sellers | Foundational physics / materials seeds |

## 2. Capital Instrument Selection Matrix

*Use in Stage 5 to select the appropriate financing instrument based on risk and venture stage.*

| Dimension | Customer Prepayments & Bootstrapping | Non-Dilutive SBIR / STTR Grants | Venture Debt / Asset-Backed | Institutional Venture Capital (VC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost of Capital** | Lowest (zero equity dilution) | Zero dilution (high compliance cost) | Moderate (interest + small warrant) | Highest (substantial equity dilution) |
| **Loss of Governance Control** | None (founder retains 100% control) | None (government takes no board seats) | Minimal (loan covenants & liens) | High (board seats, veto provisions) |
| **Tolerance for Technical Risk** | Low (customers demand working products) | Very High (explicitly funds feasibility) | Zero (requires cash flow & collateral) | High (funds high-uncertainty breakthroughs)|
| **Speed to Capital Access** | Immediate upon contract closing | Slow (6–12 month federal grant review) | Fast (30–60 days upon revenue proof) | Moderate (3–6 months pitching & DD) |
| **Ideal Venture Stage** | MVP validation & initial pilots | TRL 1–4 early laboratory discovery | TRL 7–9 post-revenue scaling | TRL 5–8 aggressive market scaling |

## 3. Go-to-Market (GTM) Entry Route Matrix

*Use in Stage 3 & 4 to navigate the entrant-incumbent asymmetry (Prof. Vish Krishnan & Dr. Rana el Kaliouby).*

| Strategy Route | Strategic Mechanism | Primary Advantage | Existential Hazard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct Autonomous Disruption** | Build internal sales force; sell directly to end-users. | Full economic value capture; direct customer relationship. | Market antibodies; incumbent distribution monopolies; high CAC burn. |
| **OEM Channel Partnering** | License software/hardware to established players (e.g., selling vision AI through GE/Siemens PACS). | Instant access to global installed base and hospital relationships. | Subsumed into incumbent catalog; sluggish partner sales force; margin compression. |
| **Joint Development Agreement (JDA)** | Co-develop commercial product with a corporate anchor customer. | Non-dilutive R&D funding; guaranteed initial customer adoption. | IP assignment ambiguity; product tuned too specifically to one client's idiosyncratic needs. |
| **Corporate Venture Spin-Off** | Spin out as independent entity with corporate anchor backing. | Autonomy from incumbent bureaucracy + established channel support. | Competitors of parent corporate refuse to transact with spin-off. |
