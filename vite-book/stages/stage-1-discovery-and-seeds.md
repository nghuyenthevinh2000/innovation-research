# Stage 1: Scientific Discovery & The Technology Seed

> **Phase Focus:** Evaluating Raw Laboratory Discoveries, Assessing Early TRL (1–3), and Escaping the Invention-Centric Mindset.

---

## 1. Stage Objective & Theoretical Foundation

Stage 1 begins in the research laboratory (academic institution, corporate R&D center, or defense lab). The objective is to translate an observable scientific anomaly or breakthrough into a defined, repeatable **Technology Seed** while avoiding the delusion that invention alone guarantees commercial value.

> **The Lab Reality**  
> Over **75% of issued patents never generate a single dollar of commercial revenue**. Scientific discovery is merely the opening ante; commercialization requires building the delivery vehicle that brings it to market. (Karim Lakhani & Vish Krishnan).

---

## 2. Key Concepts & Definitions

- **Technology Seed:** A novel laboratory discovery, material formulation, proprietary algorithm, or technological capability seeking commercial applications.
- **Invention vs. Innovation:** Invention creates the technical capability; innovation creates and captures economic value in the marketplace.
  $$\text{Innovation} = \text{Invention} \times \text{Commercialization Architecture}$$
- **Technology Readiness Levels (TRL 1–3):**
  - *TRL 1:* Basic scientific principles observed and reported.
  - *TRL 2:* Technology concept and application formulated.
  - *TRL 3:* Analytical and experimental critical function / characteristic proof-of-concept demonstrated on a lab bench.

---

## 3. Core Transformation Activities

1. **Isolate the Phenomenon:** Separate the fundamental scientific mechanism (e.g., graphene oxide ambient filtration, mRNA software programming) from incidental laboratory equipment or setup artifacts.
2. **Establish Repeatability:** Prove that the effect can be replicated consistently under varying ambient conditions, documenting boundary limits and failure modes.
3. **Draft Initial Claims:** Secure intellectual property protection (provisional patent filings) without overly constraining future cross-domain applications.
4. **Conduct Early Patent Landscape Check:** Determine whether foundational blocking patents already dominate the space before committing years to benchtop development.

---

## 4. Governing Mental Models & Failure Modes

- **Mental Model:** *Biological Evolution of Ideas* ($\text{Variation} + \text{Selection} + \text{Iteration}$). Allow broad exploratory variation before locking into an engineering roadmap.
- **Cognitive Trap to Avoid:** *The "Hammer in Search of a Nail"* — technical founders fall in love with the elegance of their discovery and assume the market will adapt to their preferences.
- **Cognitive Trap to Avoid:** *The Reasonableness Trap* — do not allow academic peer-review committees to kill audacious "what-if" hypotheses simply because they contradict conventional wisdom.

---

## 5. Stage Gate 1: Exit Deliverables

Before advancing to [**Stage 2: Problem Representation**](./stage-2-problem-framing-and-ph.md), the venture must possess:
- [ ] Documented Laboratory Proof-of-Concept (TRL 3 benchtop demonstration).
- [ ] Provisional patent application filed with broad functional claims.
- [ ] Preliminary assessment of physical boundaries (temperature, pressure, power, yield limits).
- [ ] Explicit commitment by the founding team that the technology is a *means*, not the commercial goal.
