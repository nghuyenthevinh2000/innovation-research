# Redirecting Technology: Acemoglu & Johnson's Solutions to Perpetual Displacement

Source: Daron Acemoglu & Simon Johnson, *Power and Progress: Our Thousand-Year Struggle Over Technology and Prosperity* (PublicAffairs / Hachette, 2023). Primarily Chapter 9 ("Artificial Struggle") and Chapter 11 ("Redirecting Technology").  
Companion files: [`technology-trap-perpetual-displacement.md`](./technology-trap-perpetual-displacement.md), [`technology-trap-ar-bessen-model.md`](./technology-trap-ar-bessen-model.md), [`technology-trap-sync-forces.md`](./technology-trap-sync-forces.md).

## 1. The Core Premise: Technology Is Not Destiny

The fundamental argument of *Power and Progress* is that **perpetual displacement is not an unavoidable law of technical progress, but the consequence of an institutional and ideological choice over the *direction* of innovation.**

Throughout a thousand years of history—from medieval crop rotations to the cotton gin, industrial textile mills, and early computing—technological breakthroughs routinely enriched an oligarchy and disempowered workers whenever elites had total narrative and political control over technology's path.

Prosperity only became broadly shared when society developed **countervailing power** to actively redirect innovation toward technologies that complement humans rather than replace them.

## 2. Why Conventional Solutions Fail

Acemoglu and Johnson explicitly reject the two standard policy reflexes:

### 2a. Retraining Alone Cannot Solve Velocity-Driven Displacement

Conventional economics treats technology as an unchangeable weather phenomenon and instructs workers to adapt via education. But when innovation cycles every 3–5 years:

* Workers chase an upward-moving target.
* Educational curricula take 2–4 years to update, guaranteeing that workers are trained for yesterday's frontier.
* Adapting to an anti-worker technological vector will always leave workers behind. **Society must reshape the vector itself.**

### 2b. Universal Basic Income (UBI) Is a Defeatist Trap

Acemoglu and Johnson strongly oppose UBI as the primary remedy for AI displacement:

1. **Concedes Economic Redundancy:** It accepts the premise of Silicon Valley that ordinary human beings have no productive future and must become dependent wards of the state.
2. **Destroys Political Bargaining Power:** Civic voice and political standing historically derive from workers' role in the productive apparatus. Welfare recipients without economic leverage cannot constrain authoritarian surveillance or oligarchic power.
3. **Eats Public Budgets:** A meaningful UBI would require massive tax extraction that would crowd out public investments in healthcare, early education, and R&D for clean technology.

## 3. The Three-Pronged Architecture of Redirection

To show that redirecting technology is feasible, the authors examine two historical precedents:

* **The Progressive Era (1890s–1910s):** How muckraking journalists (Ida Tarbell), civil society, and legal reformers broke the robber-baron trusts, ended unfettered corporate control of government, and built the foundation for the mid-20th-century middle class.
* **The Renewable Energy / Climate Movement (1970s–Present):** How solar and wind went from 10–20× more expensive than coal/gas in the 1990s to cheaper than fossil fuels today—driven by carbon taxes, research subsidies, emission regulations, and a narrative shift.

They propose the same three prongs for digital technologies and AI:

                          │    1. NARRATIVE & SOCIAL NORMS      │

## 4. Concrete Policy Levers (Chapter 11)

### 4a. Equalize the Tax Treatment of Capital vs. Labor

* **The Current Distortion:** The US tax code taxes labor income at roughly **25%**, but taxes corporate investments in automation equipment, hardware, and software at **less than 5%** (via accelerated depreciation, interest deductions, and credits).
* **The Remedy:** End the massive tax subsidy on machines. Level effective tax rates on labor and capital, eliminating the artificial fiscal incentive for corporations to automate tasks that humans perform efficiently.

### 4b. Subsidize "Machine Usefulness" (Human-Complementary AI)

Just as governments heavily subsidized renewable energy R&D (e.g., DARPA, NREL, German Feed-in Tariffs), public funding must subsidize classes of AI that create **new tasks (L3 Reinstatement)**:

* **Education:** AI tools providing real-time cognitive diagnostic feedback to teachers, enabling personalized classroom instruction and creating richer teaching roles.
* **Healthcare:** Diagnostic and clinical support tools that allow nurses and medical technicians to perform complex screening and procedural care.
* **Skilled Trades & Manufacturing:** Real-time troubleshooting and augmented-reality assistants that elevate line workers into complex maintenance roles.

### 4c. Tax Parity for Worker Training

* Companies receive immediate tax write-offs when purchasing automated software, but investments in **workforce retraining and on-the-job human capital** receive no comparable federal tax credit.
* Introduce tax credits for employer-sponsored worker training, modeled after the German dual-track apprenticeship system, to drastically compress $L4$ (the skill-matching lag).

### 4d. Antitrust and Breaking the Algorithmic Monoculture

* Dominant Big Tech firms impose a centralized paradigm oriented around **data harvesting, behavioral ad targeting, and pure worker replacement**.
* Antitrust enforcement is necessary not simply to lower prices, but to **ensure technological diversity**. Breaking tech monopolies allows alternative, human-centric software and decentralized paradigms room to compete.

### 4e. Data Ownership and Privacy Rights

* Establish user data ownership and strict consent frameworks to undermine the surveillance-based business model.
* Impose a **tax on digital advertising** to curb the attention-extraction arms race.
* Reform or repeal **Section 230** of the Communications Decency Act to hold platform publishers legally responsible for the automated dissemination of misinformation and extreme content.

### 4f. Institutional Worker Voice in Technology Adoption

* Technology deployment inside firms cannot remain an uncontested managerial fiat.
* The authors advocate for new organizational models—such as German-style **works councils** or shop-floor digital organizing (e.g., the Amazon Labor Union in Staten Island)—where workers co-determine how technologies are integrated into workflows, ensuring tools empower rather than surveil and displace.

## 5. Mapping the Solutions to the AR-Bessen Model

| Framework Component | Unchecked Trajectory (Perpetual Displacement) | Acemoglu & Johnson's Redirection (*Power and Progress*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **L1: Productivity Ramp** | Hyper-accelerated and subsidized by tax codes; pure replacement. | **Normalized:** Tax parity removes artificial incentives to substitute capital for labor. |
| **L2: Needs Catch-up** | Saturated in mature sectors ($\varepsilon < 1$); no domestic recovery. | Savings redirected toward human-centered public and social needs. |
| **L3: New Task Reinstatement** | Minimal or restricted to elite software/math researchers ("another fellow"). | **Maximized via subsidies for Machine Usefulness (MU):** Broad new tasks created for median workers. |
| **L4: Skill Match** | Infinite lag ($L4 \to \infty$); workers chase a perpetually moving frontier. | **Compressed:** Firm-level training tax credits and apprenticeships match workers to new tasks. |
| **Pain Gap ($PG$)** | **Permanent and rolling.** | **Closed:** Technological advance is tethered to human task creation. |
