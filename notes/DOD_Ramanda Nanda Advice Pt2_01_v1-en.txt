RAMANA NANDA: What I like to tell my students
is that they're never going to be lawyers.
So they're never going to be getting into the nitty-gritties
of these contracts between venture capitalists and themselves.
But as they think about the raising funding,
they should be thinking about the bigger picture questions.
What are the incentives that this counterparty of mine has?
Who are they beholden to?
Where do they make their money?
Where are the points of friction between them and myself going to lie?
And can I assure myself that, as we build this company together,
we're going to be aligned in all of those things?
So I think that's one set of questions that are not particularly deeply
financial, but are very important in thinking
about having a successful partnership with your financiers.
I think the other thing that people underestimate
is the importance of thinking about business models
and how they impact the cash flow of the business
and how that might have a fundamental effect on both
the amount of external finance you raise and the terms at which you
raise that money.
And so while it may seem like it's just accounting or it's just strategy,
it actually turns out to be an incredibly powerful way of also shaping
your financing strategy, because what your business model is,
what your product market strategy is will shape who you can get money from
and what your financing strategy is will ultimately also
have an impact on what your product market strategy will be.
And so thinking about those as joint decisions and thinking about those two
together, I think, is a very important thing
to be thinking about as you're building a company.
