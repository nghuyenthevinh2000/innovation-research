## Core Foundations

### `Innovation vs Invention`
- **Aliases:** `[[Inventing vs Launching Breakthroughs]]`, `Invention vs Innovation`, `Inventing vs Launching`
- **Definition:** Invention is the creation of a novel, non-obvious technical capability or scientific discovery (over 75% of issued patents never generate commercial revenue). Innovation is the successful translation of a technical capability into captured economic and societal value in the marketplace.
- **Formula / Axiom:** $\text{Innovation} = \text{Invention (Capability)} \times \text{Commercialization (Business Architecture)}$
- **Originators:** Karim Lakhani, Vish Krishnan, Joseph Schumpeter.
- **Common Trap:** *The Better Mousetrap Fallacy* — believing superior physics automatically creates customer demand without addressing switching friction.
- **Related Terms:** [[The Valley of Death]], [[Technology Seed]], [[Business Architecture]].

### `The Valley of Death`
- **Aliases:** `Valley of Death`, `Commercialization Gap`
- **Definition:** The perilous funding and operational chasm between laboratory proof-of-concept (TRL 3–4) and self-sustaining commercial revenue (TRL 8–9). Over 90% of science-based startups perish here due to premature scaling, market irrelevance, or capital exhaustion.
- **Stage:** Transition from Stage 0/1 to Stage 5/6.
- **Antidote:** Staged milestone financing, discriminating experiments, and accelerated needs-seeds synchronization.
- **Related Terms:** [[TRL]], [[Discriminating Experiment]], [[The Patience Dilemma]].

### `Emergent Innovation (Emergence)`
- **Aliases:** `[[Innovation Ideas are emergent]]`, `Emergence`, `Noubar Afeyan Methodology`, `Biological Evolution of Ideas`, `Biological Evolution Algorithm`
- **Definition:** The operational paradigm positing that breakthrough ideas are not invented by heroic individuals in sudden "aha" moments; they emerge algorithmically through continuous variation, extreme selection pressure, and iterative survival.
- **Axiom:** $\text{Breakthrough Emergence} = \text{Variation} + \text{Selection Pressure} + \text{Systematic Iteration}$
- **Originator:** Dr. Noubar Afeyan (Founder & CEO, Flagship Pioneering; Moderna).
- **Core Insight:** Retrospective bias makes breakthroughs look revolutionary; in reality, they are the result of hundreds of discarded micro-hypotheses.
- **Related Terms:** [[Reasonableness Trap]], [[Expert-Free Zone]], [[The Killer Experiment]].

### `The Reasonableness Trap`
- **Aliases:** `Reasonableness Filter`, `Reasonableness Gravitational Pull`
- **Definition:** The systemic institutional bias in academia, venture capital, and corporate committees that systematically filters out transformative breakthroughs because they sound "unreasonable" against current orthodoxies.
- **Mechanism:** Review boards fund safe, incremental proposals ("faster horses"), ensuring truly revolutionary ideas are starved of early exploration.
- **Originator:** Noubar Afeyan.
- **Related Terms:** [[Expert-Free Zone]], [[Cognitive Diversity]].

### `Expert-Free Zone`
- **Definition:** A structured psychological and organizational environment where teams can formulate audacious, seemingly absurd "what-if" hypotheses without reputational risk or immediate dismissal by incumbent domain specialists.
- **Operational Rule:** Engage non-experts to generate expansive variation; bring domain experts in strictly to provide forensic selection pressure on fundamental physical limits.
- **Related Terms:** [[Emergent Innovation]], [[Cognitive Diversity]].

### `The Five Big Ideas`
- **Aliases:** `[[Lab to Market - Five Big Ideas]]`, `Five Big Ideas`, `Harvard Technology Entrepreneurship Framework`
- **Definition:** The foundational curriculum architecture of Harvard's Technology Entrepreneurship program: (1) Solve a real problem; (2) Generate lots of ideas; (3) Innovate on models; (4) Test with customers; (5) Use money as a tool.
- **Originators:** Karim Lakhani, Vish Krishnan.
- **Related Terms:** [[Business Architecture]], [[P-H Pair]], [[Customer Validation]].

### `The Late-Joiner Trap`
- **Definition:** An organizational dilemma occurring when a team member joins mid-stream and proposes a compelling alternative hypothesis that challenges sunk investments, causing analytical deadlock.
- **Protocol:** The *Structured Challenge Protocol* — depersonalize, check if it solves the same problem, execute a 1-week empirical sprint or pre-mortem.
- **Related Terms:** [[Pre-Mortem Protocol]], [[Execution Risk vs Assumption Risk]].

## Stage 1 & 2: Problem Representation & Cognitive Search

### `Perspective`
- **Definition:** An internal coordinate system, mental model, or representational frame that dictates how a problem space is categorized, visualized, and decomposed.
- **Originators:** Herbert Simon, Scott Page, Karim Lakhani.
- **Example:** Cartesian $(x,y)$ vs Polar $(r,\theta)$ coordinates; mental arithmetic vs 3×3 Magic Square.
- **Related Terms:** [[Heuristic]], [[P-H Pair]], [[Representational Trap]].

### `Heuristic`
- **Definition:** A search algorithm, rule of thumb, or navigational strategy applied to a problem space once it has been framed through a specific perspective to locate an optimal solution.
- **Related Terms:** [[Perspective]], [[P-H Pair]].

### `Perspective-Heuristic Pair (P-H Pair)`
- **Aliases:** `[[Problem Solving Framework - Perspective and Heuristic]]`, `Perspective-Heuristic Framework`, `P-H Framework`, `P-H Pairs`, `P-H Search Space`, `P-H Search`
- **Definition:** The coupled combination of a representation and a search rule. The choice of P-H pair determines whether an innovation problem is computationally intractable, agonizingly slow, or effortlessly trivial.
- **Formula:** $\text{Problem Difficulty} = f(\text{Perspective}, \text{Heuristic})$
- **Canonical Proof:** The Herbert Simon "Number 15" game (brute-force combinatorial arithmetic vs instant Tic-Tac-Toe visual alignment via Magic Square).
- **Related Terms:** [[Magic Square Analogy]], [[Cross-Domain Transfer]].

### `Magic Square Analogy`
- **Aliases:** `Number 15 Game`, `Simon Magic Square`
- **Definition:** The demonstration by Herbert Simon that the "Number 15" game (selecting numbers 1–9 to sum to 15) is mathematically isomorphic to Tic-Tac-Toe. Shifting perspective from arithmetic to a spatial 3×3 grid converts a high-cognitive-load problem into a trivial game with zero arithmetic.
- **Lesson:** When stuck, do not work harder or compute faster; re-map the coordinate system.
- **Related Terms:** [[Perspective-Heuristic Pair]], [[Representational Trap]].

### `Representational Trap (Functional Fixedness)`
- **Aliases:** `Functional Fixedness`, `Representational Inertia`
- **Definition:** The cognitive failure mode where innovators stare at a problem in its native domain representation, attempting to brute-force a solution through optimization rather than importing a superior coordinate system from another field.
- **Related Terms:** [[Cross-Domain Transfer]], [[Cognitive Diversity]].

### `Cognitive Diversity`
- **Aliases:** `Diversity Trumps Ability`, `Scott Page Theorem`
- **Definition:** Differences in perspectives, heuristics, interpretations, and mental representations across a group of problem solvers. Scott Page's *Diversity Trumps Ability Theorem* mathematically proves that a cognitively diverse group outperforms a homogeneous group of high-ability experts on complex landscapes.
- **Related Terms:** [[Crowdsourced Search]], [[Expert-Free Zone]].

### `Cross-Domain Transfer`
- **Aliases:** `Cross-Domain Innovation`, `Model Porting`
- **Definition:** The systematic importation of a mature perspective or mathematical heuristic from an unrelated scientific discipline into a novel problem space (e.g., Brownian motion and thermodynamics applied to Black-Scholes option pricing; electrical circuit oscillation applied to epileptic brain stimulation).
- **Related Terms:** [[Functional Analysis for Seeds]], [[P-H Pair]].

### `Crowdsourced Search (Open Innovation)`
- **Aliases:** `[[Crowd Sourcing Perspective and Heuristic]]`, `Crowdsourcing Perspectives and Heuristics`, `Open Innovation at Scale`, `Broadcast Search`
- **Definition:** Broadcasting an abstract functional problem formulation to an open, global, multidisciplinary network to discover uncorrelated P-H pairs, transforming serendipity into a systematic organizational capability.
- **Cases:** Colgate-Palmolive submicron powder (solved via electrostatic charge by an outsider); NASA solar storm prediction (solved by a retired radio engineer).
- **Originator:** Karim Lakhani (Harvard LISH / InnoCentive research).
- **Related Terms:** [[The Boundary Problem]], [[Solver Profile]].

### `The Boundary Problem`
- **Aliases:** `Organizational Boundary Trap`, `Cognitive Homogeneity`
- **Definition:** The structural limitation of corporate and academic R&D departments where hiring homogeneity results in internal teams sharing identical mental models and hitting identical cognitive roadblocks.
- **Related Terms:** [[Crowdsourced Search]], [[Cognitive Diversity]].

## Stage 3: Needs-Seeds Synchronization & Functional Analysis

### `Technology Seed (Supply Push)`
- **Aliases:** `Technology Push`, `Technology Seed`, `Seed Technology`
- **Definition:** A novel laboratory discovery, material property, proprietary algorithm, or technological capability seeking commercial applications.
- **Risk:** "A hammer in search of a nail."
- **Related Terms:** [[Customer Need]], [[Accelerated Innovation]], [[Functional Analysis for Seeds]].

### `Customer Need (Demand Pull)`
- **Aliases:** `Market Pull`, `Demand Pull`, `Customer Need`
- **Definition:** A validated, quantified point of friction, workflow bottleneck, or unmet operational objective experienced by end-users.
- **Risk:** "The Faster Horse Trap" — customer myopia producing incremental, pedestrian benchmarks.
- **Related Terms:** [[Technology Seed]], [[Jobs-to-Be-Done]], [[Outcomes-Driven Innovation]].

### `Accelerated Innovation`
- **Aliases:** `[[Seeds - Needs Matching]]`, `Seeds and Needs`, `Closed-Loop Innovation`, `Contact Sport Model of Commercialization`
- **Definition:** The continuous, closed-loop synchronization of supply-side technology seeds with demand-side customer needs. Refuses to treat commercialization as a linear relay race.
- **Maxim:** *"Relying on technology-push or market-pull alone is like clapping with one hand."* (Vish Krishnan).
- **Case Study:** Via Separations (Shreya Dave: shifting graphene oxide membrane from water desalination to industrial thermal separation).
- **Related Terms:** [[Technology Seed]], [[Customer Need]], [[Functional Analysis for Seeds]].

### `Functional Analysis (Functional Thinking)`
- **Aliases:** `[[Functional Analysis for Seeds]]`, `Functional Thinking`, `Universal Function Decomposition`
- **Definition:** A systematic deconstruction methodology that strips away proprietary domain jargon and physical components to articulate what a technology *does* at an elementary physical level.
- **Core Axiom:** *Function is the goal; technology is merely a means.* Customers never buy a product for what it is, but for what it does.
- **Originator:** Dr. Sam Kogan (Gen5 Group).
- **Related Terms:** [[Main Parameters of Value (MPV)]], [[S-A-O Triad]].

### `Main Parameters of Value (MPV)`
- **Aliases:** `Main Parameters of Value`, `MPV Analysis`, `Key Parameters of Value`, `MPV`
- **Definition:** The critical 1–2 quantifiable performance or economic metrics that govern customer purchase decisions.
- **Formula:** $\text{Value} = \frac{\text{Perceived Utility / Performance}}{\text{Total Cost of Acquisition \& Ownership}}$
- **Criteria:** Must be **Main** (decisive, non-negotiable), **Parameter** (objectively measurable in physical or monetary units), and **Value** (delivers high ROI).
- **Related Terms:** [[Functional Analysis for Seeds]], [[Jobs-to-Be-Done]].

### `Subject-Action-Object (S-A-O) Triad`
- **Aliases:** `S-A-O Syntax`, `SAO Triad`, `Function Triad`
- **Definition:** The semantic syntax used in functional analysis to define technological capabilities without domain bias: an active verb applied to a target object (e.g., *"separates liquid"*, *"stops impurities"*, *"directs heat"*).
- **Use Case:** Conducting cross-industry patent searches to discover unexploited application verticals.
- **Related Terms:** [[Functional Analysis for Seeds]].

### `Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD)`
- **Aliases:** `JTBD`, `Job to be Done`
- **Definition:** The fundamental task, operational progress, or emotional outcome a customer is trying to accomplish in a specific circumstance. While technologies evolve rapidly, the underlying job remains stable over decades.
- **Classic Maxim:** *"People don't want a quarter-inch drill bit; they want a quarter-inch hole."* (Theodore Levitt).
- **Originators:** Clayton Christensen, Anthony Ulwick.
- **Related Terms:** [[Outcomes-Driven Innovation]], [[Customer Activity Chain]].

### `Outcomes-Driven Innovation (ODI)`
- **Aliases:** `[[Approaches to Uncovering Hidden Customer Needs]]`, `ODI`, `Uncovering Hidden Customer Needs`
- **Definition:** A structured customer discovery methodology that defines customer requirements as discrete metrics measuring how users assess value when getting a job done.
- **Syntax:** Direction of improvement + Unit of measurement + Object of control + Context (e.g., *"Minimize the time required to detect micro-calcifications in high-density breast tissue"*).
- **Originator:** Anthony Ulwick.
- **Related Terms:** [[Jobs-to-Be-Done]], [[Customer Activity Chain]].

### `Customer Activity Chain`
- **Aliases:** `Linkages-Driven Innovation`, `Activity Chain`, `Customer Journey Map`
- **Definition:** The sequential end-to-end journey of operational actions a customer executes to achieve an outcome (e.g., Search $\rightarrow$ Evaluate $\rightarrow$ Purchase $\rightarrow$ Install $\rightarrow$ Operate $\rightarrow$ Maintain $\rightarrow$ Dispose).
- **Originator:** Prof. Vish Krishnan (*Linkages-Driven Innovation*).
- **Analytical Vector:** Analyzes functional, economic, informational, and emotional friction at every single link.
- **Related Terms:** [[Linkages-Driven Innovation]], [[ODI]].

## Stage 4: Business Architecture & Model Alignment

### `Business Architecture`
- **Aliases:** `[[Business-Operation Model]]`, `Business and Operating Model`, `Business Architecture Framework`
- **Definition:** The comprehensive system design of a venture consisting of two interdependent pillars: the **Business Model** (value creation and capture) and the **Operating Model** (value delivery engine).
- **Axiom:** $\text{Business Architecture} = \text{Business Model} + \text{Operating Model}$
- **Canonical Warning:** Xerox PARC invented the GUI, mouse, and Ethernet, but failed commercially because they lacked the business architecture that Apple and Microsoft deployed.
- **Related Terms:** [[Business Model]], [[Operating Model]], [[Architectural Misalignment]].

### `Business Model`
- **Aliases:** `Value Creation and Capture`, `Business Model Design`
- **Definition:** The architectural pillar detailing why customers choose the offering (**Value Creation**) and how the enterprise captures a share of that economic value as sustainable revenue (**Value Capture**).
- **Related Terms:** [[Value Creation]], [[Value Capture]], [[Operating Model]].

### `Value Creation`
- **Aliases:** `Customer Value Proposition`, `Economic Surplus Creation`
- **Definition:** The mechanism by which an offering delivers economic, operational, or emotional surplus to the customer. Strategies include differentiation, cost advantage, vertical focus, and direct/indirect network effects.
- **Related Terms:** [[Value Capture]], [[Main Parameters of Value]].

### `Value Capture`
- **Aliases:** `Monetization Model`, `Revenue Model`
- **Definition:** The monetization mechanism by which an enterprise extracts a portion of the value surplus it creates.
- **Models:** Unit margin (cost-plus), licensing (IP rights), subscription (ARR), pay-per-use (metered), transaction fees, revenue sharing, and outcomes-based pricing.
- **Related Terms:** [[Value Creation]], [[Business Model]].

### `Operating Model`
- **Aliases:** `Delivery Engine`, `Operating Architecture`
- **Definition:** The delivery engine that operationalizes what was promised in the business model. Composed of three core elements: **Structure**, **Capabilities**, and **Assets**.
- **Formula:** $\text{Operating Model} = \text{Structure} + \text{Capabilities} + \text{Assets}$
- **Related Terms:** [[Business Model]], [[Business Architecture]].

### `Structure (Operating Model)`
- **Aliases:** `Operating Structure`, `Firm Structure`
- **Definition:** The organizational and commercial form of the venture: a **Product Firm** (software/hardware widget), a **Service Firm** (bespoke consulting/engineering), or a **Platform / Matchmaker** (multi-sided marketplace).
- **Related Terms:** [[Operating Model]], [[Capabilities]], [[Assets]].

### `Capabilities (Operating Model)`
- **Aliases:** `Core Competencies`, `Organizational Capabilities`
- **Definition:** The specialized knowledge, technical proficiencies, and organizational processes that enable a venture to execute its value proposition and maintain competitive differentiation (e.g., regulatory engineering, supply-chain choreography, rapid AI fine-tuning).
- **Related Terms:** [[Operating Model]], [[Assets]].

### `Assets (Operating Model)`
- **Aliases:** `Strategic Assets`, `Proprietary Assets`
- **Definition:** The unique tangible, informational, and intellectual property resources controlled by the venture (e.g., patents, proprietary longitudinal datasets, clinical trials, production tooling).
- **Related Terms:** [[Operating Model]], [[Capabilities]].

### `Architectural Misalignment`
- **Aliases:** `Business Architecture Misalignment`, `Model Disconnect`
- **Definition:** A fatal venture flaw where the promises made in the business model cannot be delivered by the structure, capabilities, or assets of the operating model (e.g., promising ultra-low-cost commodity pricing while operating a high-touch, bespoke consulting labor force).
- **Related Terms:** [[Business Architecture]], [[Operating Model]].

## Stage 5: Technology Readiness & Cloverleaf Due Diligence

### `Technology Readiness Levels (TRL)`
- **Aliases:** `[[Technology Readiness and Commercialization]]`, `TRL`, `TRL Scale`, `Technology Readiness Assessment`
- **Definition:** A 9-level milestone metric originally developed by NASA and DoD to assess the maturity and operational robustness of a technology from basic principles (TRL 1) to flight-proven commercial deployment (TRL 9).
- **Milestone Sweet Spot:** TRL 4–6 is the critical inflection zone where benchtop prototypes transition to real-world operational environments.
- **Related Terms:** [[Cloverleaf Framework]], [[The Valley of Death]].

### `The Cloverleaf Framework`
- **Aliases:** `Cloverleaf Diagnostic`, `Cloverleaf Model`
- **Definition:** A multidimensional diagnostic tool used to stress-test technology commercialization across four interdependent leaves: **Technology & IP Readiness**, **Market Need & Customer Value**, **Business Model Defensibility**, and **Execution & Operational Feasibility**.
- **Originators:** Vish Krishnan, Karim Lakhani.
- **Rule:** A breakthrough on Leaf 1 (Technology) is useless if Leaf 3 (Monetization) or Leaf 4 (Regulatory/Supply Chain) is blocked.
- **Related Terms:** [[TRL]], [[Freedom to Operate (FTO)]].

### `Freedom to Operate (FTO)`
- **Aliases:** `Freedom to Operate`, `FTO`, `FTO Analysis`, `Patent Clearance`
- **Definition:** The legal determination that practicing, manufacturing, or commercializing a technology will not infringe upon valid, active third-party patent claims or regulatory exclusivity rights.
- **Distinction:** Having a granted patent does *not* grant FTO; an innovator can patent an improvement while still infringing the foundational patent.
- **Related Terms:** [[Cloverleaf Framework]], [[Blocking Patents]].

### `Market Antibodies`
- **Definition:** The aggressive defensive mechanisms deployed by entrenched incumbents, legacy channel partners, and regulatory bodies to suppress, delay, or kill disruptive innovations (e.g., patent litigation, exclusive distributor lockouts, regulatory lobbying).
- **Related Terms:** [[10x Imperative]], [[The Valley of Death]].

### `The 10x Imperative`
- **Aliases:** `10x Imperative`, `Order of Magnitude Advantage`, `10x Advantage`
- **Definition:** The principle that to overcome incumbent market antibodies and customer switching inertia, a new technological offering must deliver an order-of-magnitude (10x) improvement along at least one critical MPV (cost, speed, yield, or convenience). A 15–20% improvement is insufficient to induce switching.
- **Related Terms:** [[Market Antibodies]], [[MPV]].

### `The Patience Dilemma (The Early Pioneer Trap)`
- **Definition:** The danger of entering a market too early when a breakthrough technology requires decades of compounding infrastructure and materials improvements before commercial viability (e.g., 3D printing pioneers failing in 1989–1995 despite the technology ultimately succeeding in 2015).
- **Related Terms:** [[TRL]], [[Valley of Death]].

## Stage 6: Customer Discovery & Deep-Tech MVP Prototyping

### `The 100-Interview Funnel`
- **Aliases:** `[[Customer Validation and Systematic Feedback]]`, `Customer Validation and Systematic Feedback`, `Customer Discovery Funnel`, `Systematic Customer Feedback`
- **Definition:** The rigorous customer discovery standard requiring early-stage founders to conduct at least 100 deep, unvarnished qualitative interviews with prospective buyers, operators, and ecosystem stakeholders (specifically strangers, never friends or family) prior to capital deployment.
- **Source:** Techstars / Harvard Entrepreneurship curriculum.
- **Related Terms:** [[The Mom Test]], [[Disconfirming Evidence]].

### `Disconfirming Evidence`
- **Aliases:** `Disconfirming Feedback`, `Hypothesis Invalidation`
- **Definition:** Data, customer responses, or experimental outcomes that invalidate the founder's core hypotheses. Skilled entrepreneurs actively search for disconfirming evidence early when capital burn is low, rather than seeking polite vanity affirmations.
- **Related Terms:** [[The Mom Test]], [[Discriminating Experiment]].

### `The Mom Test Principles`
- **Aliases:** `The Mom Test`, `Rob Fitzpatrick Principles`
- **Definition:** A conversational interview framework developed by Rob Fitzpatrick to extract authentic customer truth: (1) Talk about their life and past behaviors, not your idea; (2) Ask about specific instances in the past, never hypothetical futures; (3) Listen more than you speak; (4) Deflect compliments.
- **Related Terms:** [[The 100-Interview Funnel]], [[Hypothetical Bias]].

### `Hypothetical Bias`
- **Aliases:** `Polite Affirmation Bias`, `Hypothetical Validation Trap`
- **Definition:** The customer discovery fallacy where asking *"Would you buy a product that does X?"* generates polite, affirmative responses because hypothetical affirmations cost the customer nothing and produce false-positive validation.
- **Related Terms:** [[The Mom Test]], [[The Dog Food Test]].

### `Minimum Viable Product (MVP) in Deep Tech`
- **Aliases:** `[[Minimum Viable Products for Deep Tech]]`, `Deep Tech MVPs`, `Deep-Tech MVP Archetypes`, `Rapid Prototyping in Deep Tech`
- **Definition:** The minimum engineered artifact, simulation, or structured experience that enables a venture team to collect the maximum amount of validated learning regarding customer willingness to adopt with the least resource expenditure. Not a substandard commercial release, but a behavioral experiment.
- **Archetypes:** Conceptual MVP, Simulated MVP (Wizard of Oz), and Prototypical MVP.
- **Originators:** Karim Lakhani, Tim Miano, Eric Ries.
- **Related Terms:** [[Wizard of Oz MVP]], [[Discriminating Experiment]].

### `Simulated MVP (Wizard of Oz / Mechanical Turk)`
- **Aliases:** `Wizard of Oz MVP`, `Mechanical Turk Architecture`, `Simulated MVP`
- **Definition:** An MVP archetype where the front-end user experience appears fully automated and intelligent, while human operators manually execute backend workflows behind the scenes.
- **Purpose:** Validates customer demand and workflow integration *before* spending millions to build complex hardware, AI pipelines, or automated backends.
- **Related Terms:** [[Conceptual MVP]], [[Prototypical MVP]].

### `Think-Aloud Protocol`
- **Aliases:** `Cognitive Walkthrough`, `Think-Aloud Usability`
- **Definition:** A usability and customer discovery method where prospective users narrate their live stream of consciousness while interacting with an MVP artifact or workflow simulation, exposing authentic confusion, cognitive friction, and unarticulated needs.
- **Related Terms:** [[Minimum Viable Product (MVP) in Deep Tech]].

## Stage 7: Venture De-Risking & Capital Architecture

### `The Firm as a Cash Engine`
- **Aliases:** `[[Cash Engine and Working Capital Architecture]]`, `[[Entrepreneurial Finance Framework]]`, `Lemonade Stand Framework`, `The Firm as a Cash Engine`, `Cash Engine`
- **Definition:** The fundamental economic representation of an enterprise as a transformation machine that takes cash, converts it into operating assets, and converts those assets back into cash plus a return.
- **Formula:** $\text{Cash} \xrightarrow{\quad\text{Build/Buy}\quad} \text{Assets} \xrightarrow{\quad\text{Sale/Delivery}\quad} \text{Cash} + \Delta\text{Cash}$
- **Originator:** Prof. Ramana Nanda (Harvard Business School).
- **Core Directives:** (1) Maximize conversion margin ($\Delta\text{Cash}$); (2) Minimize capital trapped in intermediate operational assets.
- **Related Terms:** [[Working Capital Architecture]], [[Cash Conversion Cycle]].

### `Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)`
- **Aliases:** `Working Capital Cycle`, `CCC`
- **Definition:** The duration in days required to convert cash invested in operations (inventory, R&D, labor) back into cash collected from customers.
- **Formula:** $\text{CCC} = \text{Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)} + \text{Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)} - \text{Days Payables Outstanding (DPO)}$
- **Strategic Ideal:** Achieving a *Negative Working Capital* cycle (e.g., customer prepayments, upfront annual subscriptions) where customers fund company operations before suppliers must be paid.
- **Related Terms:** [[The Lemonade Stand Model]], [[Customer Financing]].

### `The Lemonade Stand Model`
- **Aliases:** `Lemonade Stand Thought Experiment`, `Three Payment Architectures`
- **Definition:** Ramana Nanda's foundational thought experiment illustrating how business models dictate external financing needs: Model 1 (Cash-on-Delivery: $\$100$ initial capital); Model 2 (Net-30 Invoice / Accounts Receivable: requires $\$3,000$ working capital debt/equity); Model 3 (Negative Working Capital / Pre-orders: $\$0$ external capital required).
- **Lesson:** Accounting profitability does not equal solvency; cash timing dictates venture survival.
- **Related Terms:** [[The Firm as a Cash Engine]], [[Working Capital Architecture]].

### `Discriminating Experiment (The Killer Experiment)`
- **Aliases:** `[[Venture De-Risking, Discriminating Experiments, and Staged Financing]]`, `The Killer Experiment`, `Discriminating Experiments`, `De-Risking Tough Tech`
- **Definition:** An empirical test specifically engineered to decisively invalidate a core venture hypothesis at the lowest possible cost and earliest possible date. If the experiment fails, the hypothesis is abandoned; if it succeeds, it unlocks a substantial valuation inflection.
- **Originators:** Ramana Nanda, Noubar Afeyan.
- **Related Terms:** [[Staged Financing]], [[Venture De-Risking]].

### `Staged Financing (Milestone Financing)`
- **Aliases:** `Milestone Financing`, `Tranche Financing`
- **Definition:** The venture capital practice of providing only enough capital to reach the next discrete discriminating milestone (TRL jump, clinical readout, regulatory clearance, commercial pilot) rather than capitalizing the entire project upfront.
- **Economic Logic:** Protects investors against downside uncertainty while allowing founders to preserve equity dilution at progressively higher valuations.
- **Related Terms:** [[Discriminating Experiment]], [[Venture De-Risking]].

### `Debt vs. Equity Risk Asymmetry`
- **Aliases:** `[[Debt vs. Equity - Risk Asymmetry and Capital Selection]]`, `Debt vs Equity`, `Capital Selection Asymmetry`
- **Definition:** The fundamental structural difference between capital instruments:
  - **Debt:** Capped upside (principal + fixed interest) with full downside risk; demands collateral, cash flow predictability, and liquid liquidation value.
  - **Equity:** Uncapped upside participation with full downside risk; tolerates unproven business models, intangible assets, and binary outcomes.
- **Implication:** Early-stage deep tech cannot be funded with traditional debt because there are no steady cash flows or tangible assets to repossess.
- **Related Terms:** [[Venture Debt]], [[Institutional Venture Capital]].

### `Non-Dilutive Capital (SBIR / STTR Grants)`
- **Aliases:** `[[Founder Bandwidth, Non-Dilutive SBIR Grants, and Advanced Customer Commitments]]`, `SBIR Grants`, `STTR Grants`, `Non-Dilutive Capital`
- **Definition:** Government research grants (e.g., Small Business Innovation Research) that fund high-risk exploratory technical feasibility without taking founder equity or board control seats.
- **Phase Sequence:** Phase I (Feasibility / proof-of-concept, $\sim\$150\text{k}–\$300\text{k}$) $\rightarrow$ Phase II (Full prototype / commercialization readiness, $\sim\$1\text{M}–\$2\text{M}$) $\rightarrow$ Phase III (Commercial deployment via DoD/agency procurement).
- **The Founder Bandwidth Dilemma:** Grants require intensive compliance reporting; pursuing grants misaligned with commercial GTM drains vital founder bandwidth.
- **Related Terms:** [[Advanced Customer Commitments]], [[Cash Engine]].

### `Cash Flow Rights vs Control Rights`
- **Aliases:** `Investor Rights Bundles`, `Governance vs Economics`
- **Definition:** The two primary bundles of legal rights negotiated in equity financings:
  - **Cash Flow Rights:** Dictate how proceeds are distributed (liquidation preferences, participating preferred shares, anti-dilution ratchets, dividends).
  - **Control Rights:** Dictate who governs venture decisions (board seats, protective veto provisions over financings, executive hires, IP transfers, or M&A).
- **Principle:** *"Money is not just money — it comes attached to strings and investor governance."* (Ramana Nanda).
- **Related Terms:** [[Institutional Venture Capital]], [[Investor Syndicates]].

## Stage 8: Scaling, Spin-Offs, Syndicates & Standards

### `Institutional Venture Capital vs Angel Investors`
- **Aliases:** `[[Institutional Capital, VC Fund Incentives, and Investor Syndicates]]`, `[[History and Evolution of Risk Capital]]`, `[[Introduction to Venture Financing]]`, `Venture Capital Dynamics`, `The Hits Business`
- **Definition:** The structural divergence in capital origin, fund horizon, and return expectations between individual angels (personal wealth, flexible horizon) and institutional VCs (fiduciary duty to LPs, 10-year fund horizon, power law economics).
- **Related Terms:** [[The Power Law ("The Hits Business")]], [[10-Year Fund Lifecycle]].

### `The Power Law ("The Hits Business")`
- **Aliases:** `Power Law Returns`, `The Hits Business`, `Venture Return Distribution`
- **Definition:** The mathematical distribution governing venture capital returns, where a tiny minority of investments ($\sim 5\%$) generate the overwhelming majority ($\sim 80\%+$) of total fund profits.
- **Implication:** Institutional VCs cannot invest in modest $\$30\text{M}–\$50\text{M}$ outcomes; every portfolio company must have the theoretical potential to return the entire fund ($>\$1\text{B}$ outcome).
- **Related Terms:** [[10-Year Fund Lifecycle]], [[Institutional Venture Capital]].

### `10-Year Fund Lifecycle`
- **Aliases:** `VC Fund Horizon`, `10-Year Fund Mandate`
- **Definition:** The rigid legal structure of traditional VC partnerships (2–3 years investment deployment $\rightarrow$ 5–7 years portfolio scaling and follow-on $\rightarrow$ fund liquidation and cash return to LPs).
- **Friction:** Creates acute tension with deep-tech ventures whose capital-intensive physics or clinical trials exceed the 10-year window.
- **Related Terms:** [[Institutional Venture Capital]], [[The Patience Dilemma]].

### `Investor Syndicate Dynamics & Signaling Risk`
- **Aliases:** `[[Seeking Investment and Investor Relationship Dynamics]]`, `Signaling Risk`, `Investor Syndicates`
- **Definition:** The collaborative structure where multiple venture firms co-invest in a financing round led by a lead investor.
- **Signaling Risk:** When an insider or lead investor with privileged information decides *not* to participate in a subsequent financing round, signaling to the outside market that the company is flawed, often triggering a catastrophic funding freeze.
- **Related Terms:** [[Institutional Venture Capital]], [[Cash Flow Rights vs Control Rights]].

### `Tech Transfer Office (TTO) Pitfalls`
- **Aliases:** `[[Venture Acceleration and Spin-Off Pitfalls]]`, `Spin-Off Pitfalls`, `University Tech Transfer Traps`
- **Definition:** Common hazards encountered when licensing technology out of universities or national laboratories: aggressive upfront cash demands, excessive royalty drag on gross margins, unassigned intellectual property, and toxic cap tables (passive academic founders holding majority control).
- **Antidote:** Negotiating milestones tied to commercial success, clean IP assignment, and equity splits reflecting operational commitment.
- **Related Terms:** [[Freedom to Operate]], [[Venture Acceleration]].

### `Standards War`
- **Aliases:** `[[Case Study - Qualcomm CDMA Commercialization]]`, `[[Go-to-Market Foundations]]`, `Qualcomm CDMA Story`, `Standards Battles`
- **Definition:** An existential commercial battle where rival technological architectures compete to become the de facto industry standard (e.g., CDMA vs TDMA/GSM, Betamax vs VHS, AC vs DC).
- **Case Study:** Qualcomm CDMA commercialization: overcoming industry consensus, running the Dallas shoot-out demonstration, and pivoting from pure licensing to chip and system manufacturing to force global standard adoption.
- **Related Terms:** [[Market Antibodies]], [[Go-to-Market Foundations]].
